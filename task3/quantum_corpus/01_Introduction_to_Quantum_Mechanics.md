# 1. Introduction to Quantum Mechanics and the Crisis of Classical Physics

At the end of the 19th century, classical physics was considered a nearly complete science. However, two unresolved problems remained: the ultraviolet catastrophe and the photoelectric effect.

Classical electrodynamics predicted that a perfectly black body should emit an infinite amount of energy at high frequencies, contradicting the laws of conservation of energy. In 1900, Max Planck proposed that energy is emitted not continuously but in discrete packets called quanta.

The energy of a quantum is calculated using the formula $E = h\nu$, where $h$ is Planck's constant and $\nu$ is the radiation frequency. This discovery laid the foundation for a new era in physics.
