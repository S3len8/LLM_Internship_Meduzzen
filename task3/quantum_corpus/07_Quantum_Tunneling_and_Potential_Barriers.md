# 7. Quantum Tunneling and Potential Barriers

Quantum tunneling is a process in which a microscopic particle passes through a potential barrier whose energy exceeds the particle's kinetic energy.

In classical physics, the particle would be reflected by such a barrier. In quantum physics, because of the particle's wave properties, its wave function does not become zero inside the barrier but decays exponentially. If the barrier is thin enough, there is a nonzero probability of finding the particle on the other side.

The tunneling effect underlies alpha decay in atomic nuclei, the operation of scanning tunneling microscopes, and the operation of Zener semiconductor diodes.
