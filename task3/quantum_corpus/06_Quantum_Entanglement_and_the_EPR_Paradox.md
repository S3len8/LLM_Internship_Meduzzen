# 6. Quantum Entanglement and the EPR Paradox

Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon in which the quantum states of two or more particles become correlated regardless of the distance between them.

In 1935, Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen proposed the EPR paradox, questioning the completeness of quantum mechanics through the idea of "spooky action at a distance." They believed that hidden parameters existed.

In 1964, John Bell developed a mathematical theorem known as Bell's inequalities. Experimental tests by Alain Aspect confirmed quantum-mechanical predictions and disproved the theory of local hidden parameters.
