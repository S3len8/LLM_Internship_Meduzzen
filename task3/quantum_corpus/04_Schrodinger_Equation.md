# 4. Schrödinger Equation and the Wave Function

In 1926, Erwin Schrödinger proposed a fundamental equation that describes how the state of a quantum system changes over time.

The state of a quantum particle is described by the wave function $\psi(x, t)$. The time-independent Schrödinger equation has the form $\hat{H}\psi = E\psi$, where $\hat{H}$ is the Hamiltonian operator representing the total energy of the system.

Max Born explained the physical meaning of the wave function: the squared magnitude of the wave function $|\psi(x, t)|^2$ determines the probability of finding the particle at position $x$ at time $t$.
