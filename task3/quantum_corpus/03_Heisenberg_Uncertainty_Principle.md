# 3. Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

Formulated by Werner Heisenberg in 1927, this principle is one of the cornerstones of quantum mechanics.

The uncertainty principle states that it is impossible to measure the position and momentum of a quantum object precisely at the same time. Mathematically, this is expressed by the relation $\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}$, where $\hbar = \frac{h}{2\pi}$.

This limitation is not an imperfection of measuring instruments but a fundamental feature of the quantum world. An analogous relation exists for energy and time: $\Delta E \cdot \Delta t \ge \frac{\hbar}{2}$.
